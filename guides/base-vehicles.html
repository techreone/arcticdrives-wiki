Arctic Drive Base Vehicle Guide: Trailer vs Fixed Base

Compare a camper, a trailer and a fixed garage in Arctic Drive, with setup steps, running costs and the point where a mobile base stops paying.

Last Updated: 2026-11-27

Quick answer: A camper is a home you can drive; a trailer is a warehouse you tow; a fixed garage is the cheapest workshop you can build. Start fixed, add a trailer for resupply, and only move to a full-time camper when your route is long enough to justify the fuel.

The truck is already a shelter, but a base is where the loop stops being reactive. You need a powered bench, a recycler, a dry bed and a parking spot that does not trap you in a drift. The official feature list offers two routes: hook up a trailer or an RV for a mobile base, or build out a fixed shelter with a generator. The crafting guide→ covers the workshop; this page covers where to put it.

What counts as a base vehicle in Arctic Drive?

Three things qualify:

Route choice decides whether the base is reachable at all; the route reading guide→ covers ice, bends and whiteouts.

  • The truck — seats, storage, heat and the only way out. It is the base until you build something better.
  • The camper or RV — a driveable shelter with a bed and interior space. It can be parked as a forward base.
  • The trailer — a towed cargo body with shelves and a ramp. It is not a shelter by itself, but it can carry the workshop.

A fixed base is any shelter you improve: a cabin, garage or stash room with a generator, heat, lights and a bench. The distinction that matters is not wheeled versus static. It is whether you can leave without packing.

Mobile base vs fixed base: which is cheaper?

Factor Camper / RV Trailer Fixed garage
Setup cost High: vehicle, fuel, repairs Medium: trailer plus hitch Low to medium: shelter plus generator
Fuel burn Continuous, because you drive it Added drag on every leg Zero when parked
Repair exposure Crashes, tires, engine, interior Hitch, door, wheels, cargo Storm damage only
Storage Limited but always with you High, but vulnerable High and organized
Best for Long routes, no safe garage Resupply and base moves Crafting, storing, staging
Main risk Stranding your home Losing the trailer on a bend Getting snowed in

The cheapest setup is a fixed garage with a generator and a bench, plus the truck for day trips. The most flexible is a camper. The worst of both is a trailer packed with everything you own and no fixed place to sort it.

The two-base rule

Keep one fixed workshop and one mobile kit. The fixed base holds the recycler, spare parts and overflow cargo; the mobile kit holds one repair kit, one jerrycan, food and warm clothes.

How do you set up a camper as a mobile base?

  1. Park level and nose-out. A camper on a slope slides, and a blocked exit turns a shelter into a trap.
  2. Check the heater before you unpack. Heat is the reason to use a camper instead of a tent.
  3. Secure loose items. The v0.23.2 patch fixed cargo falling under the car when loading a save; it did not make shelves self-closing.
  4. Keep the driving kit in the cab. Repair kit, torch, food and one jerrycan should never be buried under base cargo.
  5. Mark the spot. If you leave the camper, note the landmark so you can find it in a whiteout.

The v0.23.0 patch fixed players being ejected from the camper at high speed in co-op and improved the shelves. That makes the camper more usable at speed, not indestructible. Slow down before a bend with passengers in the back.

How do you use a trailer without losing it?

A trailer is a force multiplier on a known road and a liability on an unknown one. Use it for base moves and resupply, not for exploration.

  • Attach before loading. A loaded trailer is harder to line up with the hitch.
  • Use the ramp. The v0.23.0 trailer ramp saves time and saves your back.
  • Hand-pull the last metre. Get out, use the hitch, and position the trailer without forcing the truck into a tight spot.
  • Drop it before a steep descent. A trailer that starts steering the truck is already too heavy for the grade.
  • Check the door after every stop. A trailer door that opens on the road empties your base.

Do not put your only repair kit in the trailer. If the trailer is lost, the truck should still be able to fix itself.

What does a fixed base need?

A useful fixed base has five things:

  1. Heat — a stove, heater or generator-powered room.
  2. Power — generator fuel for lights and the recycler.
  3. A bench — crafting and research in one place.
  4. Storage — a stash room for parts, food and overflow cargo.
  5. A clean exit — a parking spot you can leave without reversing into a drift.

The v0.23.2 patch fixed the stash room's generator, heat and lights, which makes the prefab garage a stronger first base than it was at launch. Check that the generator actually runs before you move your whole stock inside.

When should you switch from fixed to mobile?

Switch when the drive from your base to the content is longer than the content itself. If you spend 20 minutes commuting and 10 minutes looting, a camper pays for itself. If you spend 10 minutes commuting and an hour crafting, the fixed base is cheaper.

A good rule: stay fixed until you have a full repair kit, a spare wheel and a known fuel stop on the far side of the map. Then move the mobile kit, not the whole base.

FAQ

Can you sleep in a trailer?

The trailer is cargo space, not a heated shelter. Sleep in the truck, the camper or a cabin. A trailer parked next to a cabin is useful storage, not a bedroom.

Does a camper use the same fuel as a truck?

It burns fuel when driven and needs repairs like any vehicle. That continuous cost is why a fixed base is cheaper for short routes.

Can you tow a trailer with a camper?

The game supports trailer hookups, but a camper plus trailer is a long, heavy combination. Test it on a straight road before you take it into the hills.
