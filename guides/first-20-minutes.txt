<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Spend the first 20 minutes making the truck livable, not looting every shelf. Install the battery, keep one jerrycan and one repair kit, load heavy cargo low, then drive to the nearest cabin before dark. Drop decorations and empty boxes first; they cost fuel and buy you nothing.</p>
<p>The opening is a packing problem disguised as a driving game. Your truck is the shelter, the storage locker and the only way out, so every kilogram you add is a kilogram the engine has to pull through snow. The <a href="/guides/hauling-and-fuel" class="guide-action-capsule" data-link-type="internal-capsule">hauling and fuel guide<span class="capsule-arrow">→</span></a> covers the math in detail; this page is the 20-minute version you can run while the engine is warming up.</p>

<h2 id="what-should-you-put-in-the-truck-before-you-leave">What should you put in the truck before you leave?</h2>
<p>Load for the first night, not for a theoretical week. The truck has a battery slot, a fuel tank, a cargo bed and a cab; the cab is also your warm space, so do not turn it into a warehouse.</p>
<p>If a part does break, the <a href="/guides/repair-and-damage" class="guide-action-capsule" data-link-type="internal-capsule">repair priority guide<span class="capsule-arrow">→</span></a> tells you what to fix before you spend scrap.</p>

<p>The battery is the item new players forget. If the engine turns over and dies, or the dash stays dark, check the battery before draining the tank.</p>
<h2 id="how-should-you-load-the-truck-in-the-first-hour">How should you load the truck in the first hour?</h2>
<p>Put the heaviest single items on the floor of the bed or trunk, directly over the rear axle. Put lighter boxes on the back seat, and keep jerrycans upright in the trunk where they cannot slide into the cab. Close the tailgate before you move; loose cargo shifts under braking and can spill on a tight turn.</p>
<p>The official store page is blunt: weight burns fuel faster, and tight turns can spill the haul. Leave one empty seat and floor space for a repair kit or a food box.</p>
<div class="callout callout-tip"><div class="callout-title">Load once, not three times</div><div class="callout-body"><p>Carry the first load only as far as your first shelter. Unload what you need for the night, then repack in daylight when you can see what you actually have.</p></div></div>
<h2 id="where-should-you-spend-the-first-night">Where should you spend the first night?</h2>
<p>A cabin with a stove is the best first-night shelter. It gives you heat, a place to dry out and a save point that does not depend on fuel. A garage or stash room with a generator is the second choice; it is warmer than the cab and usually has a workbench nearby. The truck is the fallback, not the plan.</p>
<p>Drive to the shelter while you still have daylight. Park nose-out, close to the door, and leave the driver's side clear. If the cabin has a generator, start it before you unload so the lights and heat are already running. Then bring in food, water, one repair kit and your bedroll, and leave the rest locked in the truck.</p>
<p>Do not sleep through a whiteout in an unheated cab. The game tracks body temperature and hypothermia; get to heat first and sort inventory second.</p>
<h2 id="what-can-you-throw-away-immediately">What can you throw away immediately?</h2>
<p>Throw away anything bulky with no mechanical job: decorative items, duplicate broken parts, empty boxes and low-value clutter that came with the truck. Empty boxes are recyclable, so if a recycler is close, turn them into materials instead of carrying them for 20 km. Broken parts are also recyclable, but keep one spare of the part that strands you most often.</p>

<p>If the cabin is farther than your reserve allows, stop at a gas station first. Stations carry jerrycans, and parked cars can be siphoned. Do not push a 25 km route on a quarter tank.</p>
<h2 id="what-should-you-do-on-night-one">What should you do on night one?</h2>
<ol>
<li>Park facing the exit and set the handbrake.</li>
<li>Start the generator or stove before unloading.</li>
<li>Bring in food, water, one repair kit and warm clothes.</li>
<li>Repair the part that is closest to failing, not the cosmetic damage.</li>
<li>Check the map for the next fuel stop and mark the route you will drive at first light.</li>
<li>Save, then sleep.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
<h3 id="can-you-play-the-first-night-without-a-cabin">Can you play the first night without a cabin?</h3>
<p>Yes, but you need the cabin heater and a parking spot out of the wind. Keep the engine off while you sleep unless you have a reason to run it, and check the temperature bar before you lie down.</p>
<h3 id="should-you-take-the-trailer-on-day-one">Should you take the trailer on day one?</h3>
<p>No. A trailer doubles the number of things that can go wrong before you have a repair kit, a spare and a known route. Hook one up after your first full fuel run.</p>
