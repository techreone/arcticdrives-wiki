<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Recycle empty boxes and broken parts into materials, spend the first scrap on a repair kit, then a cargo box, then vehicle mods. Keep the bench, recycler and generator in one shelter so every return trip turns junk into range instead of clutter.</p>
<p>Arctic Drive's crafting loop is not a side activity. It is how a truck that limped into a cabin leaves with a repaired radiator, a spare kit and a turbo that makes the next climb possible. The official page describes recycling empty boxes, broken parts and scrap into materials, then crafting upgrades and spare parts at a bench. The <a href="/guides/repair-and-damage" class="guide-action-capsule" data-link-type="internal-capsule">repair guide<span class="capsule-arrow">→</span></a> explains which part to fix first; this page explains how to pay for it.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-the-scrap-loop-in-arctic-drive">What is the scrap loop in Arctic Drive?</h2>
<p>The loop has four stages:</p>
<p>Where you put the bench matters as much as what you craft, and the <a href="/guides/base-vehicles" class="guide-action-capsule" data-link-type="internal-capsule">base vehicle guide<span class="capsule-arrow">→</span></a> compares the options.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Collect</strong> empty boxes, broken parts and loose scrap from cabins, stations and wrecks.</li>
<li><strong>Recycle</strong> them at a recycler to produce materials.</li>
<li><strong>Research</strong> the blueprint you actually need at the crafting bench.</li>
<li><strong>Craft</strong> the kit, box or mod and install it on the truck.</li>
</ol>
<p>The generator powers the shelter and the workshop. A bench in an unpowered shack still works for some recipes, but the recycler and lights are what make a night stop productive. Build the loop around one room and stop hoarding in the truck.</p>

<p>Do not recycle your last repair kit, your only battery or a part that matches the truck. The recycler does not know what you need tomorrow.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-blueprints-work">How do blueprints work?</h2>
<p>Blueprints unlock recipes. You research them at the bench, then craft the item with materials. The launch-window screenshot shows a tiered list with a <strong>Good Repair Kit at 100 scrap</strong>, an <strong>Awesome Repair Kit at 200 scrap</strong>, a <strong>cargo box at 35</strong> and a <strong>flare gun at 60</strong>. Those are the prices visible in that build; check your own bench before planning a shopping list.</p>
<p>Research has a cost and a time component, so do not start a long research project at dusk if you need the kit before morning. Research the repair kit first, then the cargo box, then the mods you will actually install.</p>
<div class="callout callout-warning"><div class="callout-title">The sandbox soft lock</div><div class="callout-body"><p>A sandbox start without a repair kit recipe can leave you unable to fix the first serious breakdown. If you are experimenting, research the kit before you leave the starting area.</p></div></div>

<p>Vehicle mods come in three levels. A level-one heater or extra tank is a reasonable mid-game target; a level-three turbo on a damaged engine is how you turn a repair bill into a new run.</p>
<h2 id="where-should-the-crafting-bench-live">Where should the crafting bench live?</h2>
<p>Put it in a fixed shelter with a generator, not in the truck. A mobile bench sounds efficient until you need to craft in a blizzard and the trailer is buried. The best base has:</p>
<ul>
<li>a generator with fuel,</li>
<li>a recycler within arm's reach,</li>
<li>a crafting bench with light,</li>
<li>a dry room for the night,</li>
<li>and a parking spot where the truck can leave without reversing into a drift.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you are running a mobile base, keep the bench in the trailer but do the recycling at fixed stops. The trailer is for hauling the output, not for running a factory in a whiteout.</p>
<h2 id="how-do-you-avoid-a-crafting-soft-lock">How do you avoid a crafting soft lock?</h2>
<p>Keep a minimum kit in the truck at all times: one repair kit, one battery, one spare wheel, one jerrycan, one torch. That is your insurance against a bad spawn or a recipe you have not researched. In co-op, the host can enable recipe sharing so the crew does not duplicate research.</p>
<p>The other guard is a scrap reserve. Do not spend every material on a cosmetic upgrade before a long route. A 200-scrap repair kit is cheaper than abandoning a truck full of cargo because you spent the scrap on a bull bar.</p>
<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>
<h3 id="can-you-craft-while-driving">Can you craft while driving?</h3>
<p>No. Crafting happens at a bench. Plan your crafting stops around shelter, not around the middle of a fuel leg.</p>
<h3 id="does-recycling-give-different-materials-per-item">Does recycling give different materials per item?</h3>
<p>Yes. Different items break down into different materials, which is why emptying a mixed box of junk is worth more than hauling it. Check the recycler output before committing a stack.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-craft-or-loot-repair-kits">Should I craft or loot repair kits?</h3>
<p>Loot them when you find them, craft them when you can. A found kit is free range; a crafted kit is insurance. Keep at least one of each before a long run.</p>
